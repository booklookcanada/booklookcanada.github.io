What it says it does

In its own words, as filed with CRA (from its return for the year ended Dec 31, 2024)

As a newly established charity we faced significant challenges in our first year period that is from January 1 to December 31 2024. Several external factors --including inflation increased tariffs and a general decline in charitable giving --contributed to a difficult fundraising environment. These factors impacted both public interest and the financial capacity of potential donors resulting in a lack of revenue generation for the period. Despite these challenges we have remained committed to advancing our mission and laying the groundwork for future success. We proactively conducted outreach to various potential supporters. This included formal letters sent to major philanthropic and global health organizations such as the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation the World Health Organization and all major banks in Canada among others. These organizations all share an interest in Telemedicine and Telehealth initiative around the world especially in Africa which aligns with our charity's cause. We followed up multiple times after these submissions and while we have not yet received positive responses we are still hopeful and persistent in our efforts. We understand that building trust and visibility takes time especially during a charity's initial phase. In addition to these outreach efforts we are now implementing a grassroots campaign strategy to promote monthly micro-donations We believe this sustainable approach will reduce reliance on large one-time donations and help us grow a strong and loyal donor base. While our first fiscal year presented substantial challenges it was also a critical period of learning and foundation-building. We remain optimistic that the efforts and strategic planning we undertook in 2024 will lead to a successful and impactful 2025 and beyond.
We will seek to provide free virtual healthcare services medical consultations and health education to underserved populations in Canada and abroad. The program aims to overcome geographical financial and systemic barriers to healthcare access through the use of telemedicine technology and volunteer medical professionals. Through secure online platforms the charity facilitates: Remote consultations between patients and licensed physicians nurses and mental health professionals. Health education webinars and videos on preventative care chronic disease management maternal health and mental well-being. Digital triage and follow-up care coordination for individuals in rural or marginalized communities. Training sessions for local community health workers on how to use telehealth tools. Tele-pharmacy connections for prescriptions and patient counselling. All services are offered free of charge and are supported through charitable donations grants and volunteer efforts. Key Activities: Recruiting and scheduling licensed Canadian and international healthcare professionals. Partnering with local organizations and clinics to identify patient needs. Operating a secure video and chat-based telehealth platform. Delivering patient care remotely with referrals to in-person care when needed. Providing internet/data support to patients who lack digital access. Monitoring outcomes and improving service delivery through community feedback. Target Beneficiaries: Low-income and remote populations in Canada Elderly patients with limited mobility Refugees and newcomers lacking immediate healthcare access International communities in underserved rural areas Community-based clinics without specialist access Expected Impact: Reduce healthcare inequalities by increasing access to professional consultations Improve early diagnosis and treatment outcomes Support mental health through culturally sensitive virtual counselling Promote public health education and self-care literacy
